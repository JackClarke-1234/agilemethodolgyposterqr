What is Agile?

Agile is an iterative, flexible approach to software development and project management. It emphasises collaboration, continuous delivery, customer feedback and rapid adaptation to changing requirements.

Iterative
Work is delivered in short cycles called sprints.
Collaborative
Customers and teams work together throughout.
Adaptive
Change is expected and welcomed, not resisted.

The Agile lifecycle

A product moves through six phases. The iteration phase repeats until the product is ready to release.

  1. 1
    ConceptDefine the idea and scope
  2. 2
    InceptionForm the team and fund it
  3. 3
    IterationBuild, test and review in sprints
  4. 4
    ReleaseDeliver working software
  5. 5
    MaintenanceSupport and improve
  6. 6
    RetirementPhase out the product

Disadvantages

Lack of predictability

The scope can change every sprint based on feedback. This makes it hard for project managers to give marketing launch dates, finance budgets or delivery dates for contracts.

Team dependency

Agile relies on a skilled, motivated and self-organised team, so speed depends on the team’s knowledge and capability. If people leave, the project loses experience and velocity, and estimates become unreliable.

Suitable projects

Agile works best where requirements evolve and feedback arrives early.

Software development

Emphasises flexibility, collaboration and short feedback cycles. Teams break complex problems into manageable tasks and capture frequent feedback to align with customer needs.

Real-world example: Spotify

The Spotify model uses squads, tribes, chapters and guilds to organise teams and encourage collaboration.

SquadsSmall, cross-functional teams focused on specific features or products.
TribesCollections of squads working on related areas.
ChaptersGroups of people with similar skills across different squads.
GuildsCommunities of interest that span the entire organisation.
